# On-camera shots (phone)

The creators you named (Riley Brown, Shawn Kanungo) put a real person on camera, talking straight to the viewer.
These three short takes add that to the video. The animated screens stay as they are; the person appears on top of them.

Best on camera: a county health director or a Foundation leader, with their written consent.

## How to shoot

- Phone held upright (vertical), at eye level, about an arm's length away.
- Face a window for light. Plain wall behind. Quiet room.
- Record each line three times. Say it like you'd say it to one person, not a crowd.
- 4K or 1080p, 30 fps. Send the files as they come off the phone; no editing.

## The three takes

| Take | Where it goes | Line (12 words or fewer) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | First 2 seconds | "Your county can't fix what it can't see." |
| 2. Reframe | After the five barriers | "We count the people who show up. Not the ones who never made it." |
| 3. Ask | Before the sign-off | "Bring CB-CAP to your county. Talk to the SozoRock Foundation." |

Optional fourth take, for the Foundation's own channels: "Care. For every zip code."

## What happens next

The person's own voice replaces the synthetic voice on those lines, which also reduces how much paid voice the video needs.
The person appears in a framed box; the screens and captions keep running around them.
